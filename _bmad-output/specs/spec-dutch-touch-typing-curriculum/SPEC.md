# Dutch Touch-Typing Curriculum, First Version

## Why

Dutch children aged 10-12 need a touch-typing progression grounded in finger placement and usable Dutch text. The classic English lessons start at F/J, while the comprehensive pack introduces A-Z alphabetically; neither is a ready-made Dutch course.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1**
  - **intent:** A maintainer can compare the two English packs as starting points for Dutch curriculum design.
  - **success:** The outline contrasts key order, finger guidance, drill quality, cumulative review, and when real words become possible.
- **CAP-2**
  - **intent:** A maintainer can follow a staged Dutch touch-typing plan for ages 10-12.
  - **success:** Opening stages list new and prior keys, finger movement, practice type, and review checkpoints; the outline states how later keys should be ordered.
- **CAP-3**
  - **intent:** A maintainer can prepare and check early Dutch exercises against the keys taught so far.
  - **success:** Sample exercises distinguish purposeful key drills from Dutch words, and validation criteria reject unintroduced keys, empty or duplicate lessons, and premature punctuation.

## Constraints

- Start from F/J tactile landmarks and home-row finger placement, not alphabet order; prioritize accurate reach-and-return movements over speed.
- A lesson may use only previously taught letters and its declared new focus keys. Before natural Dutch words are possible, use clearly labelled drills rather than fabricated vocabulary or English filler.
- Target Netherlands QWERTY. Do not introduce punctuation, shift, dead keys, or accents until their mappings are established for a later stage.
- First-version acceptance uses document and automated content checks only; no human review, child testing, or physical-world verification is required. Feedback after use can inform later revisions.

## Non-goals

- Implementing or shipping the full Dutch course, changing the English packs, or expanding the existing Dutch vertical-slice milestone.
- Proving learning outcomes or settling every later key position in this first version.

## Success signal

A maintainer can take the outline, identify the next taught keys and valid exercises for each opening stage, and check that no sample asks a child to type an unintroduced key. The first version is ready to refine after actual use, without a human-review release gate.

## Assumptions

- F/J-first, short cumulative practice, and frequent retrieval are design hypotheses based on general touch-typing knowledge, not independently verified educational findings.
